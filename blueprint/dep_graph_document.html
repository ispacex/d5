Remark 2.3a bounded form

For finite groups the proof gives more. If \(G\) is finite then so are \(M=G/\gamma _4(G)\) and \(H\le M\), and one may take \(Q=H/T\), a finite \(2\)-group whose order is the \(2\)-part of \(|H|\), hence divides the \(2\)-part of \(|G|\). Therefore: if \(D_5(Q)=1\) for all metabelian \(2\)-groups \(Q\) of class \(3\) and order \(\le 2^k\), then \(D_5(G)\subseteq \gamma _4(G)\) for every finite group \(G\) whose Sylow \(2\)-subgroup has order \(\le 2^k\). This is also formally verified (§4), without hypotheses and both for \(Q\) of class \(\le 3\) and of class exactly \(3\), since Passi’s theorem and \(D_3=\gamma _3\) are formalized; the formal proof does not need Gruenberg’s theorem, as there \(H=M\) and \(Q\) is the Sylow \(2\)-subgroup of \(M\), a direct factor since \(M\) is nilpotent. Note that the exponent step uses Passi’s theorem for \(H\) (where \(\gamma _4(H)=1\)), not for an arbitrary group.

Remark 6.7

The exponent hypothesis is used in three places: in (C) (for \(d(j)\ge 3d(i)\) Tahara’s condition is empty and terms \(u_{ij}[x_i^{d(i)},x_j^3]\) with \(x_j^3\notin \gamma _2\) appear), in the choice of basis (Tahara’s (D), (E) hold only modulo \((d(i),e(k))\), \((d(i),f(l))\)), and in (iii). Theorems 6.8 and 6.9 below remove the first two: what they need is that \(\gamma _2/\gamma _3\) has exponent \(3\). At exponent \(9\) the method breaks in several places: identity (iii) may fail, and \(\gamma _2/\gamma _3\) is no longer elementary, so the \(Y_i\) of these proofs cannot always be taken as basis elements, and (D)–(F) are taken modulo numbers that may equal \(9\). Theorem 6.10 below shows that exponent \(9\) by itself is not an obstruction: the method still works if \(\gamma _4^3=1\) and \(\gamma _2^9\subseteq \gamma _4\), and Theorem 6.11 shows that \(\gamma _4^3=1\) alone suffices; what remains is \(\gamma _4^3\ne 1\). As for (iii): in a group of class \(4\), for \(x\in Q\), \(a\in \gamma _2\) and \(c=[x,a]\), the exact formula is \([x^3,a]=c^{x^2}c^xc=[x,a]^3[x,a,x]^3\), as \([x,a,x,x]\in \gamma _5\), while \([x,a^3]=[x,a]^3\) always holds, as \([\gamma _3,\gamma _2]=1\); so (iii) holds if and only if \([x,a,x]^3=1\), which is automatic when \(\gamma _2/\gamma _3\) has exponent \(3\). For \(Q=\langle x,y\mid x^9,y^9\rangle /\gamma _5\), of order \(3^{15}\) and class \(4\), with \(\gamma _2/\gamma _3\cong C_9\), a computation gives, for \(a=[x,y]\), \([x,a,x]^3\ne 1\) and so \([x^3,a]\ne [x,a]^3\), and also \([x^9,y]=1\ne [x,y]^9\). (This \(Q\) has two generators, so it is metabelian, see the proof of Lemma 6.1, and is not a counterexample; it only shows that these identities fail.) A computational check of the theorem: \(169\) random non-metabelian groups of class \(4\) with \(Q/\gamma _2\cong C_3^3\) or \(C_3^4\), of orders \(3^{10}\)–\(3^{79}\), \(29\) of them with \(\Omega _1(Z)\cap Q''\cap M_5\ne 1\): identity (iii) holds and \(D_5=1\) in all of them (truncated Magnus algebra modulo \(3^{19}\) with the exactness certificate).

Remark 6.13the case \(\gamma _4\cong C_9\)

Steps (1)–(3) of the proof of Theorem 6.11 do not use the exponent of \(\gamma _4\), so \((\star )\) and \((\Delta )\) hold in every finite \(3\)-group of class \(\le 4\), and so do (c1)–(c3) for the basis of step (4). Remark 2.2“metabelian” is automatic

Every group of nilpotency class \(\le 3\) is metabelian: \(G''=[\gamma _2(G),\gamma _2(G)]\subseteq \gamma _4(G)\) by P. Hall’s inclusion \([\gamma _i,\gamma _j]\subseteq \gamma _{i+j}\) (see e.g. [MP09]); this is the same fact used in \((2)\Rightarrow (1)\). So in (2) and (3) the word “metabelian” may be dropped: (3) is the statement that \(D_5(Q)=1\) for every finite \(2\)-group of class \(3\), and the search space of §5 is simply the \(2\)-groups of class \(3\). The same holds for Lie rings (\([\gamma _2,\gamma _2]\subseteq \gamma _4\)), so Theorem C of [IMP26] for \(c=3\) needs no metabelian hypothesis either; the hypothesis matters only for \(c\ge 4\), where \(D_{2c-1}=1\) for all groups of class \(\le c\) is a different, stronger question. Accordingly, ANUPQ’s option Metabelian cut nothing in our searches. That groups of class \(\le 3\) are metabelian is also formally verified (§4).

Remark 5.5pruning the generation tree

Let \(Q\) be a finite \(2\)-group of class \(3\) with cyclic centre, \(d=d(Q)\), \(C=C_Q(\gamma _2(Q))\), \(X=\gamma _2(Q)/(\gamma _2(Q)\cap Z(Q))\). The commutator map \(X\times Q/C\to \gamma _3(Q)\) is a non-degenerate bilinear pairing into the cyclic group \(\gamma _3(Q)\le Z(Q)\); hence \(X\cong Q/C\), a quotient of \(Q_{ab}\), and \(X\) has rank \(\le \min (d,d(d-1)/2)\). If \(|\gamma _3(Q)|=2^e\), then \(Q_{ab}\) has two invariants \(\ge 2^e\) (the map \((u,v)\mapsto [u,v,z]\) factors through \(\Lambda ^2Q_{ab}\)) and \(X\) has an element of order \(\ge 2^e\). For a minimal counterexample \(Q\) (Lemma 5.2) with central involution \(n\) this gives conditions on every quotient \(V=Q/N\) with \(N\le \Phi (Q)\) (with \(n\in N\le \Phi (Q)\) for the second), which pass to further quotients and so may be used to cut whole subtrees of the \(p\)-group generation tree: (i) there is a cyclic \(K\), \(\gamma _3(V)\le K\le \gamma _2(V)\cap Z(V)\), with \(\mathrm{rk}(\gamma _2(V)/K)\le \min (d,d(d-1)/2)\); (ii) for such a \(K\), \(|Q|\ge 2|K|\, |A\vee B\vee E_1|\, |A\vee V_{ab}\vee E_2|\), where \(A=\gamma _2(V)/K\), \(B=V/C_V(\gamma _2(V))\), \(E_1=\mathbb {Z}/2^{e_V+1}\), \(E_2=E_1^2\), \(2^{e_V}=|\gamma _3(V)|\), and \(\vee \) is the componentwise maximum of abelian invariants. In particular every such \(V\) of class \(3\) has \(d+4e_V+2\le \log _2|Q|\). These conditions are proved by hand; the proofs are in compute/pq2048/README.md. Condition (i) is vacuous for \(d\le 3\) and below order \(2^{2d+1}\); condition (ii) is the one that cuts.
