    • Lean: Theorem 6.12, “⇐”: if $P$ ($p$ odd, $\gamma_4=1$, $|\gamma_3|=p$) is generated by $x_1,\dots,x_d$ with $|P/\gamma_2|=p^d$, a normal form $u,v,w,x_i$ is given, (N2) holds and (E)+(J) has a solution with $c=1$, then $P$ is nonterminal in the sense of Lemma C.1. With “⇒” this is the criterion relative to a given normal form. The group $Q$ is built not by a pc-presentation but by adjoining the $x_i$ one at a time to $\langle u,v,w,z\rangle$ as semidirect products with $\mathbb Z$; the Jacobi identity this needs has $z$-component (J), and its remaining part holds in $P$. Lemma C.1 is still on paper only. Lean.
    • Lean: the Lie algebra of Appendix A (Remark 6.4(2)): over any commutative ring the brackets $[e,k_i]=u_i$, $[k_1,k_2]=\rho$, $[k_1,u_2]=t$, $[k_2,u_1]=-t$, $[e,\rho]=2t$, $[e,t]=w$, $[u_1,u_2]=w$ define a Lie algebra generated by $e,k_1,k_2$; over any field it has type $(3,3,1,1)$ and $[\gamma_2,\gamma_2]\ne0$. So in the Lie analogue of Lemma 6.3 the hypothesis on $\gamma_2/\gamma_3$ (at most two generators) is sharp: three do not suffice. Lean.
    • Lean: Gruenberg's theorem in the form Proposition 2.1 needs: in a finitely generated nilpotent group, an element none of whose odd powers is $1$ survives in some finite $2$-group quotient. Along the way, finitely generated nilpotent groups are residually finite (by induction on the class). Proposition 2.1 in Lean now has one hypothesis, Passi's theorem $D_4^2\subseteq\gamma_4$. Lean.
    • Lean: Passi's theorem $D_4(G)^2\subseteq\gamma_4(G)$ for every group. The proof goes through Tahara's map $\psi_3$, already formalized for his Theorem 6.1: for $g\in D_4$ of a finite $p$-group of class $\le3$, Tahara's conditions give $g=\prod_{i\lt j}[x_i^{d(i)},x_j]^{u_{ij}d(j)/d(i)}$, and hence $g^2=1$; the general case goes through Sylow subgroups, finite generation and residual finiteness. Proposition 2.1 and Remark 2.3 are now proved in Lean without hypotheses, and Lemma 6.2 keeps one, Gupta–Tahara's theorem. Lean.
