The starting point is the kernel of the reference implementation dimsub.py (method A in numpy; it lives in a separate, so far private repository). This document covers its profile, a port to C (variant A), a different way of computing (variant B) with a proof of correctness, the comparison, timings and recommendations.

Notation: $G$ is a finite $p$-group, $x_1,\dots,x_d$ are its generators, $\Delta\subset\mathbb Z[G]$ is the augmentation ideal, $F$ is the free group on $x_1..x_d$, $\mathfrak f\subset\mathbb Z[F]$ is its augmentation ideal, $A_n=\mathbb Z\langle a_1,\dots,a_d\rangle/(\text{degree}\ge n)$ is the truncated free associative algebra, $A_n^+$ is its part without constant term, $M=\operatorname{rk}A_n^+=d+d^2+\dots+d^{n-1}$.

0. Summary

  • Variant B is correct (proof in §3) and confirmed by comparison: 0 differences from dimsub.py on every group for which answers exist (§5), and full agreement with an independent implementation of the same method, in both directions.
  • Speed. On groups of order $3^7$: numpy 80–330 s, A in C 7–22 s (×11–15), B 0.2–1.4 s (×230–380, including Python start-up). Variant B went through all 1202 candidates of order $3^7$ in 157 s on three threads (numpy: about 100 CPU hours).
  • Most importantly, B does not depend on $|G|$. The Bartholdi–Mikhailov group of order $3^{494}$ ($d=7$): $\mathbb Z[G]/\Delta^4$ in 15 s modulo $3^{19}$, the exact HNF over $\mathbb Z$ in 510 s, with the same indices. Rips-type 2-groups of orders $2^{40}$–$2^{107}$: seconds per group.
  • The limit of B is $M=\sum_{k<n}d^k$, which grows like $d^{n-1}$. For $d=6$, $n=7$ (the plateau question of [IMP26, §3.3]) $M=55\,986$, and dense linear algebra does not work there (estimate in §6.3).
  • FLINT's nmod_mat_howell_form is slower than even numpy on these matrices (§2); our own Howell form for the local ring $\mathbb Z/p^E$ is 10–15 times faster than numpy.

1. Profile of the current kernel (method A, numpy)

Method A: $\Delta\cong\mathbb Z^{|G|-1}$, $\Delta^n=\mathrm{span}\{b(x_i-1)\}$ over a basis $b$ of the lattice $\Delta^{n-1}$, Howell form modulo $p^E$ ($E=19$ for $p=3$, $30$ for $p=2$), a loop over columns in Python, rows processed in vectorised form.

  • All the time goes into the Howell form, 95–98 %. Building the blocks $b\cdot(x_i-1)$ takes a fraction of a second, the membership test a few percent.
  • The matrix at every level has the same size, $d(|G|-1)\times(|G|-1)$, and the rank is always $|G|-1$. The cost is $\sim d|G|^3/2$ operations per level. The growth of the time with $n$ comes from the $p$-torsion of $\Delta/\Delta^n$: the rows $p^{E-v}\cdot$(pivot) return to the pool.
  • In the original run over the sample of order 2187 (14 processes in parallel) the median was 278 s and the maximum 659 s. A single process is 2–4 times faster: under full load numpy is memory-bound.
  • The order $|G|^3$ is the main limit: $2^{10}$ costs 8 times more per level than 512, and a group of order $2^{38}$ cannot be enumerated at all.

2. Variant A: a port to C

c/dimsub_a.c and c/hw.h: the same algorithm, the same $E$, the same output as dimsub.analyse, in fast mode and with --full. The check "$\Delta^n$ is a right ideal" from --full is not ported.

  • Own Howell form (the default). The ring $\mathbb Z/p^E$ is local, so the pivot is the row with the least $p$-adic valuation in the column, and it divides the others: one subtraction per entry, no xgcd on pairs of rows. Rows are stored as uint32 ($p^E<2^{31}$), row -= f·piv is computed in uint64 with reduction through double ($q=\lfloor x/D\rfloor$ with error $\le1$ for $x<2^{63}$, one correction), without division. The loop vectorises (-O3 -march=native; the test machine has AVX-512DQ).
  • FLINT (--flint): nmod_mat_howell_form from FLINT 3.3 (conda-forge libflint). It is general for $\mathbb Z/N$: for each pair of rows an xgcd and a unimodular $2\times2$ transformation; moreover, it needs at least as many rows as columns. On our matrices it is slower than numpy (table in §4).
  • Delayed reduction (accumulating several row -= f·piv without taking the remainder) does not fit into 64 bits at $p^E\approx2^{31}$: each product is already $\approx2^{62}$. It makes sense only for smaller $E$ (§6).

3. Variant B: $\mathbb Z[G]/\Delta^n$ as a quotient of the truncated free algebra

3.1. Statement

Let $G=F/N$, $\pi:\mathbb Z[F]\to\mathbb Z[G]$, $\mathfrak r=\ker\pi$, and let $\mu:\mathbb Z[F]\to A_n$ be the Magnus ring homomorphism $x_i\mapsto1+a_i$ (then $x_i^{-1}\mapsto\sum_{k<n}(-a_i)^k$). Let $R_0\subset F$ normally generate $N$.

Theorem. $\mathbb Z[G]/\Delta^n\cong A_n/J_n$, where $J_n=\mathrm{span}_\mathbb Z\{\,u\,(\mu(r)-1)\,v : r\in R_0;\ u,v \text{ monomials}\}$ is the two-sided ideal generated by the elements $\mu(r)-1$. The isomorphism sends $g$ to $\mu(w_g)$ for any word $w_g$ representing $g$. Therefore $$g\in D_n(G)\iff \mu(w_g)-1\in J_n .$$ Moreover, $A_n^+/J_n\cong\Delta/\Delta^n$ is a finite $p$-group, and $\operatorname{rk}A_n^+=M$ does not depend on $|G|$.

3.2. Proof

(a) The truncated Magnus isomorphism: $\mathbb Z[F]/\mathfrak f^n\cong A_n$. $\mu$ is well defined: $1+a_i$ is invertible in $A_n$ (the inverse is $\sum_{k<n}(-a_i)^k$, since $a_i^n=0$), and $F$ is free, so $x_i\mapsto1+a_i$ extends to a homomorphism $F\to A_n^\times$, and linearly to a ring homomorphism $\mathbb Z[F]\to A_n$. The constant term of $\mu(f)$ equals the augmentation $\varepsilon(f)$: both maps are ring homomorphisms to $\mathbb Z$ that agree on the $x_i$. Hence $\mu(\mathfrak f)\subseteq A_n^+$ and $\mu(\mathfrak f^n)\subseteq(A_n^+)^n=0$, which gives $\bar\mu:\mathbb Z[F]/\mathfrak f^n\to A_n$. Conversely, by the universal property of the free associative algebra there is a ring homomorphism $\varphi:\mathbb Z\langle a\rangle\to\mathbb Z[F]/\mathfrak f^n$, $a_i\mapsto x_i-1$. A monomial of degree $\ge n$ goes to a product of $n$ elements of $\mathfrak f$, that is, to $0$, so $\varphi$ factors through $A_n$. Next, $\bar\mu\varphi(a_i)=a_i$, hence $\bar\mu\varphi=\mathrm{id}$. And $\varphi\bar\mu(x_i)=x_i$, hence $\varphi\bar\mu$ is the identity on $F$ (on $x_i^{-1}$ because a ring homomorphism preserves inverses), and $F$ additively generates $\mathbb Z[F]$. $\square$

Magnus's deep theorem ($\mu^{-1}(\deg\ge n)=\mathfrak f^n$ in $\mathbb Z\langle\langle a\rangle\rangle$) is not needed here: for the truncation the statement is elementary.

(b) $\ker\pi=\mathfrak r$ is the two-sided ideal $I$ generated by $\{r-1:r\in R_0\}$. The inclusion $I\subseteq\ker\pi$ is obvious. For $n\in N$ we have $n-1\in I$, since $wrw^{-1}-1=w(r-1)w^{-1}$, $r^{-1}-1=-r^{-1}(r-1)$ and $ab-1=a(b-1)+(a-1)$: induction on the length of $n$ as a product of conjugates of $r^{\pm1}$. Finally, $\ker\pi\subseteq\mathbb Z[F](N-1)$: if $\sum c_ff\in\ker\pi$, then the sum of the coefficients over each coset $tN$ is zero, and $\sum_{f\in tN}c_ff=t\sum c_f(t^{-1}f-1)$ with $t^{-1}f\in N$. $\square$

(b′) If $S$ generates $N$ as a subgroup (for example, $S$ the Schreier generators), then $\ker\pi=\sum_{s\in S}(s-1)\mathbb Z[F]$, and a right ideal suffices: $ns-1=(n-1)s+(s-1)$, $s^{-1}-1=-(s-1)s^{-1}$, and $\ker\pi=(N-1)\mathbb Z[F]$ by the same computation with right cosets.

(c) Putting it together. $\pi(\mathfrak f)=\Delta$ (the image of the augmentation ideal under a surjection of group rings), so $\pi(\mathfrak f^n)=\Delta^n$ and $\mathbb Z[G]/\Delta^n\cong\mathbb Z[F]/(\mathfrak r+\mathfrak f^n)$. Under $\bar\mu$ the submodule $(\mathfrak r+\mathfrak f^n)/\mathfrak f^n$ goes to $\mu(\mathfrak r)$. The image of an ideal under a surjective ring homomorphism is the ideal generated by the images of the generators, that is, $\mu(\mathfrak r)=J_n$. As an abelian group it is spanned by $u\,\mu(r-1)\,v$ with monomials $u,v$, because $A_n$ is additively generated by monomials. Finally, $g-1\in\Delta^n\iff w_g-1\in\mathfrak r+\mathfrak f^n \iff\mu(w_g)-1\in J_n$. $\square$

(d) Finiteness and exactness modulo $p^E$. $\varepsilon(J_n)=0$, so $A_n=\mathbb Z\oplus A_n^+$ and $A_n^+/J_n\cong\Delta/\Delta^n$. Multiplication gives an epimorphism $(\Delta/\Delta^2)^{\otimes k}\to\Delta^k/\Delta^{k+1}$, and $\Delta/\Delta^2\cong G_{ab}$ is a finite $p$-group. Hence all $\Delta^k/\Delta^{k+1}$ and $\Delta/\Delta^n$ itself are finite $p$-groups. Therefore the exactness certificate of dimsub.py carries over verbatim. Non-membership $\mu(w_g)-1\notin J_n+p^EA_n^+$ is always exact. Membership is exact if $p^{E-1}A_n^+\subseteq J_n+p^EA_n^+$: then the exponent of $A_n^+/J_n$ divides $p^{E-1}$ and $p^EA_n^+\subseteq J_n$. Moreover, $\log_p|A_n^+/(J_n+p^EA_n^+)|=\log_p|\Delta/(\Delta^n+p^E\Delta)|$, so the field index_logp must agree with dimsub.py. In the comparison it does.

3.3. Where the relations come from

From the multiplication table (py/magnus.py; the input is the same JSON line as for dimsub.py). A breadth-first search on the Cayley graph gives a spanning tree and words $w_g$; each edge $(g,x_i)$ gives $$\rho_{g,i}=\mu(w_g)(1+a_i)-\mu(w_{gx_i})=\big(\mu(s_{g,i})-1\big)\,\mu(w_{gx_i}),\qquad s_{g,i}=w_gx_iw_{gx_i}^{-1}.$$ The elements $s_{g,i}$ are the Schreier generators of the kernel $N$ (they generate it as a subgroup), and $\mu(w_{gx_i})$ is invertible. By (b′) $J_n=\mathrm{span}\{\rho_{g,i}\,v\}$: closure under multiplication on the right by $a_j$ suffices, and that is a shift of coordinates. No inverses are needed; all $\mu(w_g)$ are computed along the tree in $O(|G|\cdot M)$. There are $(d-1)|G|+1$ relations, but only $M$ columns, and the Howell form is accumulated in chunks.

From a pc-presentation, without enumerating the group (gap/export_pres.g → py/magnus_pres.py). GAP builds a pc-sequence refining the lower central series. Layer $k$ is a modulo pcgs of $\gamma_k/\gamma_{k+1}$, collected by sifting the commutators $[b,x_j]$ ($b$ an element of layer $k-1$) and their $p$-th powers, so that an element of layer $k$ has a word $W\in\gamma_k(F)$. The relations are: power relations $W_i^p=\prod W_j^{e_j}$, commutator relations $[W_j,W_i]=\prod W_k^{e_k}$, and $x_j=\prod W_k^{e_k}$. This is a presentation of $G$ on $x_1..x_d$ by Tietze transformations: the maps $G=\langle s\mid\text{pc}\rangle\to\Gamma$, $s_i\mapsto W_i$, and $\Gamma\to G$ are mutually inverse, which is checked directly on the $s_i$ and on the $x_j$.

Cut-off by weight. $\mu(\gamma_k(F))\subseteq1+A^{\ge k}$, by induction on $k$ from the identity $[x,y]-1=x^{-1}y^{-1}\big((x-1)(y-1)-(y-1)(x-1)\big)$. Hence a relation from $\gamma_n(F)$ gives $\mu(r)-1=0$ in $A_n$ and need not be written out. We drop the power relations of layers $\ge n$ and the commutator relations with $k_i+k_j\ge n$: their right-hand sides lie in $\gamma_{k_i+k_j}(G)$, that is, in layers $\ge k_i+k_j$. For the same reason the words of elements of layers $\ge n$ are replaced by the identity. Without this, the words of the deep layers of the group of order $3^{494}$ do not fit into 8 GB.

3.4. All levels at once

$J_n$ is the image of $J_N$ ($n<N$) under dropping the degrees $\ge n$. If the columns are ordered by increasing degree, then the rows of the Howell form of $J_N$ with pivot column of degree $<n$, truncated to degrees $<n$, form a Howell form of $J_n$. Indeed, an element of the projection that is zero before column $c$ lifts to $J_N$ with the same first coordinates and decomposes over the rows with pivot $\ge c$. The same holds for the HNF over $\mathbb Z$. magnus_pres.py computes $J_{n_{\max}}$ once.

3.5. Testing a candidate subgroup $K$ (sieve)

If $K\subseteq D_{n-1}$ is an elementary abelian $p$-subgroup with independent generators $g_1..g_r$, then $g\mapsto(g-1)\bmod\Delta^n$ is a homomorphism on $K$: $gh-1=(g-1)+(h-1)+(g-1)(h-1)$ and $(g-1)(h-1)\in\Delta^{2n-2}\subseteq\Delta^n$. Hence $D_n\cap K$ is the kernel of $c\mapsto\sum c_iv_i$ modulo $J_n$, $v_i=\mu(w_{g_i})-1$. This is a single Howell form of the matrix $[J_n\mid0;\ V\mid I;\ 0\mid pI]$: the rows with pivot in the last $r$ columns generate the kernel (magnus_pres.py --kernel). Only the words for $g_1..g_r$ are needed, not for all elements of the group.

3.6. Where B loses

Variant B has $M=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}d^k$ columns, variant A has $|G|-1$. In the sample of order $3^7$ ($d\le4$, $n\le5$) $M\le340$ versus 2186; for the surviving groups of order 512 ($d\le5$, $n\le4$) $M\le155$ versus 511. But for large $d$ and large $n$ the rank of the free algebra overtakes $|G|$: for $C_2^6$ at $n=6$ $M=9330$, for groups of order $2^{10}$ with $d=6$ at $n=5$ $M=1554>1023$. Hence two rules.

  • In the full comparison mode (all levels up to $n=6$) B stopped on groups with $M>500$ and was compared on the common prefix of levels (the field truncated; the number of such groups is in §5).
  • For each group, choose B if $M<|G|$, and A otherwise.

The real remedy is to store not the dense $A_n$ but only the "standard" monomials. With an ordering compatible with multiplication (deg-lex), a monomial that leads a row of $J$ with an invertible coefficient stays leading with an invertible coefficient in all multiples $u\,w\,v$ as well, so it is eliminated together with them. What remains is an order ideal of monomials, of size at least $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}\Delta_{\mathbb F_p}/\Delta_{\mathbb F_p}^n$ (§6.4). This is the nilpotent quotient algorithm for an associative algebra, but over the ring $\mathbb Z/p^E$ rather than over a field as in ModIsom. It is not part of the prototype (§6).

6. Recommendations

6.1. What to run

  • The original screening of the 3-groups of order $3^7$ is no longer needed: all 3-groups of order $\le3^7$ of class 4 are metabelian, and for them $D_5=\gamma_5$ by Gupta–Tahara 1985. For order $3^7$ it has been done anyway: B went through all 1202 candidates in 157 s on three threads, with $D_n=\gamma_n$ for all $n$ everywhere. Variant A in C independently gave the same (4 905 s on three threads): 1 202 groups, 6 010 levels, 0 differences from B. The point of the kernel now is large groups and 3-groups of order $3^8$ and above.
  • Orders $3^8$, $2^{10}$ and above. Do not export permutations; instead export from GAP/ANUPQ the pc-presentation and only the generators of $K$ (export_pres.g + magnus_pres.py --kernel). The cost per group is building $J_5$ ($M=30..780$ for $d=2..5$, fractions of a second to seconds) plus the export in GAP. The bottleneck is generation and the sieve in GAP, not the lattice. For $d\ge6$ and $n=5$ ($M\ge1554$) A in C over the whole group is better while $|G|\le2^{11}$.
  • The Rips group ($2^{38}$). B from a presentation fits: the group has class 3, $n=4$ is needed, and $M=\sum_{k<4}d^k\le399$ even for $d=7$. This is the same as what was already computed for $3^{494}$ and for the Rips-type family $2^{40}$–$2^{107}$ (seconds per group). What is missing is a presentation of the Rips group itself: it was not found in open sources; Mikhailov–Passi, LNM 1952, is needed. Searching for the smallest quotients means adding one relation and recomputing $J_4$, also seconds.

6.2. What to do with the kernel next

  1. Rewriting B in C/FLINT makes sense only together with the switch to standard monomials (§3.6): at the current sizes ($M\le780$) Python is not the bottleneck.
  2. A in C with one group per thread is the fallback for $M\ge|G|$.
  3. Exactness: every "$\in$" found needs a certificate (certified) or the HNF over $\mathbb Z$ (--exact).

6.3. An estimate for the plateau question of [IMP26, §3.3]: is $w\in D_7$?

$d=6$, $n=7$: $M=6+6^2+\dots+6^6=55\,986$. The heavy computation was not run.

Answered in §6.5 (17.10.2026): $w\notin D_7(G)$, certified; the dense computation on a GPU took 8 minutes.

  • Dense (as now). One Howell form $M\times M$ in uint32 is 12.5 GB, twice that with the row pool. The operation count is $\sim M^3/3\approx6\cdot10^{13}$ per pass. On one core (~$10^9$ operations/s in our C) that is 15–20 hours. But $J_7$ is generated not by $M$ but by $\sim\sum_r\sum_{t\le6-\operatorname{ord}r}(t+1)6^t$ rows: about $5\cdot10^4$ per relation with a linear part, that is, of the order of $10^6$ rows, and the closure needs several such passes. In total $10^{14}$–$10^{15}$ operations: days on the test machine, and at the limit of its memory (61 GB, shared with CI). Not recommended.
  • Standard monomials (§3.6). The number of columns would be of the order of $\log_2|\Delta/\Delta^7|$ plus the monomials with a non-invertible pivot, that is, probably thousands rather than $5.6\cdot10^4$. That is minutes. Measured in §6.4: not so, at least 23 354 columns. It needs an implementation (the nilpotent quotient of an associative algebra over $\mathbb Z/2^E$): a few days of work. The size can be estimated cheaply in advance: compute $\log_2|\Delta/\Delta^n|$ for $n=2..5$ with the current B ($M\le1554$) and extrapolate.
  • Without $\Gamma$, through $G$. If $G=M/K$ is a finite 2-group, then its pc-presentation with the weight cut-off $<7$ reduces the number of relations but not $M$. So it does not save the dense variant.

6.5. A GPU kernel for the dense Howell form modulo $2^{32}$ (prototype)

gpu/howell_gpu.py; for a free GPU, gpu/howell_colab.ipynb (Colab or Kaggle, T4 16 GB). For $p=2$, $E\le32$ the arithmetic is uint32 overflow (modulo $2^E$, $E<32$: the same and a mask).

  • Algorithm. The Howell form of §2, blocked by panels of $b=256$ columns. On a panel, only the rows that are nonzero there take part; the column steps run as in c/hw.h (pivot of least 2-adic valuation, normalised to $2^v$, exact division, the row $2^{E-v}\cdot$pivot into the pool), and every step is recorded as coefficients $G$ on the pivot rows. Then the pivot rows on the trailing columns are $(I-SG_{\rm piv})^{-1}SO$ (a unit lower triangular inverse by doubling), and every other row gets $O+G\cdot P$: one product (rows $\times b$)$\cdot$($b\times$ rest), the bulk of the work.
  • The product modulo $2^{32}$, exactly. Three backends, each checked at start against an exact integer product: u32 (integer matmul with wrap-around), f32 (bytes in float32: for the shift $s$, $\sum_{i+j=s}a_ib_j$ over 64-column chunks is $<2^{24}$, exact; 10 byte products, $i+j\le3$) and i8 (signed bytes on INT8 tensor cores through torch._int_mm; the int32 accumulator never wraps). The last is the scheme of the Ozaki-II analogy; on the CPU only the first two run.
  • Comparison. Two Howell forms of one span are compared after reduction to the reduced Howell form, which is unique: byte for byte, by SHA-256. Random matrices (every valuation, rank defects, $E$ from 1 to 30, panel widths 1–256; 1 500 cases): no differences from c/hw.h.

(a) The class-3 quotient

Lemma 1 [on paper]. Let $Q$ be a finitely generated nilpotent group of class $\le3$ and $n\in\{4,5\}$. Then $D_n(Q)\subseteq\tau(\gamma_3(Q))$ (the torsion of $\gamma_3$), and $\theta:\gamma_3(Q)\to\Delta^3/\Delta^n$, $g\mapsto g-1$, is a homomorphism with kernel $D_n(Q)\cap\gamma_3(Q)$. Hence, if $\tau(\gamma_3(Q))$ is a 2-group, $D_n(Q)=1$ iff $\theta$ is injective on the elements of order 2 of $\tau(\gamma_3(Q))$. Proof. $D_3=\gamma_3$ for every group (classical, see [MP09, ch. 1] †), so $D_n(Q)\subseteq\gamma_3(Q)$. By Jennings the rational dimension subgroups are the isolators: $D_n(Q)\subseteq\sqrt{\gamma_n(Q)}=\tau(Q)$ for $n\ge4$ (Jennings 1955 †; the same fact as Quillen's theorem behind the certificate of §6.4). For $g,h\in\gamma_3$: $gh-1=(g-1)+(h-1)+(g-1)(h-1)$ and $(g-1)(h-1)\in\Delta^6$. A nontrivial subgroup of a finite 2-group contains an element of order 2. $\square$

Corollary 2 [on paper]. Let $G$ be a group with $\gamma_5(G)=1$ and $Q=G/\gamma_4(G)$ with $D_5(Q)=1$. Then $D_r(G)\cap\gamma_4(G)=1$ implies $D_r(G)=1$ for every $r\ge5$: the image of $D_r(G)$ in $Q$ lies in $D_r(Q)\subseteq D_5(Q)=1$.

For a record $G_\varphi=M/\ker\varphi$ of §6.6 one has $G_\varphi/\gamma_4=M/\gamma_4(M)=\Gamma/\gamma_4(\Gamma)\Gamma''$: $Q$ depends only on the base presentation $\Gamma$, not on $\varphi$. So 80 groups $Q$ cover all 844 groups of §6.6.

(b) Which $K\subseteq\gamma_4(M)$ could give $w\in D_7(M/K)$: one Howell form for all of them

Let $M=\Gamma/\gamma_5(\Gamma)\Gamma''$ (class 4), $A_4=\gamma_4(M)$, $A=\mathbb Z[M]$, $\Delta$ its augmentation ideal, $n\le8$.

Lemma 3 [on paper]. (i) $\psi:A_4\to\Delta^4/\Delta^n$, $k\mapsto k-1$, is a homomorphism ($(k-1)(k'-1)\in\Delta^8$). (ii) For $K\subseteq A_4$ and $v\in M$: $v\in D_n(M/K)\iff v-1\in\Delta^n+(K-1)A$, and $$(K-1)A+\Delta^n=\Delta^n+\sum_{k}\psi(k)\cdot R,\qquad R=\mathrm{span}\{1\}\cup\{(x_{j_1}-1)\cdots(x_{j_l}-1):1\le l\le n-5\},$$ $k$ over generators of $K$ ($K$ is central, $\psi(k)\Delta^{n-4}\subseteq\Delta^n$, and $\Delta/\Delta^{n-4}$ is spanned by those monomials). Proof. The kernel of $\mathbb Z[M]\to\mathbb Z[M/K]$ is $(K-1)A$, and $\Delta(M/K)^n$ is the image of $\Delta^n$. For $k=\prod k_j^{e_j}$, $\psi(k)\equiv\sum e_j\psi(k_j)$ by (i). $\square$

So for a fixed $K$ the question $w\in D_n(M/K)$ is linear: add the rows $\psi(k_j)\cdot r$ to $J_n(M)$. The existence of some $K$ is not: with $\Lambda(k\otimes r)=\psi(k)r$, a $K$ works iff some solution $(k_r)_r\in A_4^{R}$ of $\sum_r\psi(k_r)r\equiv\psi(w)$ (an affine coset of $\ker\Lambda$) has $w\notin\langle k_r\rangle$. "The coordinates of a point of a coset generate a subgroup that misses $w$" is a union over the subgroups $K$ (equivalently over $\varphi\in\widehat{A_4}$ with $\varphi(w)\ne0$), not an intersection: dually, a counterexample is a $\varphi$ that is not of the form $\varphi=\chi\circ\Lambda(\cdot\otimes1)$ for a character $\chi$ of $A/J_n$ with $\chi\circ\Lambda\in\langle\varphi\rangle\otimes\widehat R$ — a rank-one condition on a tensor, which is not linear in $\chi$. This is where a single linear problem stops. What does survive is one-sided:

Proposition 4 [on paper]. Let $U_n=J_n+\Lambda(A_4\otimes R_+)$, $R_+$ the monomials of degree $1..n-5$, and $\bar\psi:A_4\to A/U_n$. If $\bar\psi$ is injective, then $D_n(M/K)\cap\gamma_4(M)/K=1$ for every $K\subseteq A_4$. Proof. If $v\in D_n(M/K)\cap A_4$, $v\notin K$, then by Lemma 3 $\psi(v)\in J_n+\psi(K)+\Lambda(K\otimes R_+)\subseteq U_n+\psi(K)$, so $\bar\psi(vk^{-1})=0$ for some $k\in K$, and $v=k\in K$. $\square$

Conversely, $\ker\bar\psi$ contains $D_n(M)\cap A_4$ (take $K=1$), and every $v\in D_n(M/K)\cap A_4$, $v\notin K$, gives $1\ne vk^{-1}\in\ker\bar\psi$ for some $k\in K$ — the test is a filter: it can only fail where a counterexample could be. Two practical points:

  • $\ker\bar\psi$ is torsion: $\Lambda(A_4\otimes R_+)\subseteq\Delta^5$, so $z\in\ker\bar\psi$ gives $z\in D_5(M)\subseteq\sqrt{\gamma_5(M)}=\tau(M)$ (Jennings), so it is enough to test the elements of order 2 of $\tau(A_4)$ (all of them at once: socle_bits $=$ their number); injectivity found modulo $2^{32}$ is exact (as in (a)). Since $\ker\bar\psi_{n+1}\subseteq\ker\bar\psi_n$, injectivity at $n$ gives it at every larger $n\le8$.
  • With $D_4(M/\gamma_4)=1$ from (a) and Corollary 2: $\bar\psi_n$ injective $\Rightarrow$ $D_n(M/K)=1$ for every $K\subseteq\gamma_4(M)$ ($n\le7$).

The ring (§6.7, last paragraph). $L'_N$: the free metabelian Lie ring on $x_1..x_4$, Hall order $x_3<x_1<x_2<x_4$, $t=[x_4,x_3,\dots,x_3]$ ($N$ letters $x_3$), $c_i=[t,x_i]$, $u_i=[c_i,x_i]$, the relators $r_1=4x_1+2c_3+c_2$, $r_2=16x_2+4c_3-c_1$, $r_3=64x_3-4c_2-2c_1$, (ii) $\gamma_{N+4}=0$, (iii) every Hall basis element of weight $N+3$ other than $u_1,u_2,u_3$ is $0$, (iv) $u_1=16u_3$, $u_2=4u_3$, $64u_3=0$; $a=32[x_1,x_2]+64[x_1,x_3]+128[x_2,x_3]$. Grading: with $\deg x_1=\deg x_2=\deg x_3=1$, $\deg x_4=-N$ every relator is homogeneous ($\deg c_i=\deg x_i$), so everything splits into components; $a$ has degree 2. (a) The decomposition

Notation. $G$ any group, $\Delta$ its augmentation ideal, $X_i=x_i-1$ for $x_1,x_2,x_3\in G$; $A$ an antisymmetric integer matrix with $h_{ij}:=x_j^{A_{ij}}\in\gamma_k(G)$ ($i\ne j$); $g_i=\prod_{j\ne i}h_{ij}$, $u_{ij}=[x_i,h_{ij}]$ ($[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$), $v_i=\prod_{j>i}u_{ij}$, $w=\prod_{i<j}u_{ij}=[x_1,x_2^{A_{12}}][x_1,x_3^{A_{13}}][x_2,x_3^{A_{23}}]$ (the $w$ of (3.12), of §6.7, of L2g), and $$S=\sum_iX_i(g_i-1),\qquad X\!V=\sum_iX_i(v_i-1),\qquad B_{ij}=\sum_{m\ge2}\Big[\tbinom{A_{ij}}m X_j^mX_i+\tbinom{-A_{ij}}m X_jX_i^m\Big].$$

Lemma 7 [on paper]. $w-1\equiv S-X\!V-\sum_{i<j}B_{ij}\pmod{\Delta^{2k}}$. Proof. $(h-1)(h'-1)\in\Delta^{2k}$ for $h,h'\in\gamma_k$, so $g_i-1\equiv\sum_j H_{ij}$ with $H_{ij}=h_{ij}-1$. From $x_ih=hx_i[x_i,h]$: $X_iH_{ij}-H_{ij}X_i=hx_i(u_{ij}-1)=(u_{ij}-1)+(X_i+H_{ij}+H_{ij}X_i)(u_{ij}-1)$, and $u_{ij}\in\gamma_{k+1}$, so $X_iH_{ij}\equiv H_{ij}X_i+(u_{ij}-1)+X_i(u_{ij}-1)$. Next $H_{ij}X_i+X_jH_{ji}=\sum_{m\ge1}\binom{A}mX_j^mX_i+\sum_{m\ge1}\binom{-A}mX_jX_i^m$ ($A=A_{ij}$, $H_{ji}=x_i^{-A}-1$; for a negative exponent the binomial series is finite modulo $\Delta^{2k}$), and the terms $m=1$ cancel: this is $B_{ij}$. Summing over $i<j$: $S\equiv\sum_{i<j}(u_{ij}-1)+X\!V+\sum B_{ij}$; finally $\sum_{i<j}(u_{ij}-1)\equiv w-1$ since $(u-1)(u'-1)\in\Delta^{2k+2}$, and $\sum_{j>i}X_i(u_{ij}-1)\equiv X_i(v_i-1)$. $\square$

No hypothesis on $G$ beyond $h_{ij}\in\gamma_k$ (not even metabelian). In a Lie ring the analogue is exact with $X\!V=B=0$: $a=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}x_ix_j=\sum_ix_ig_i$ [IMP26, (3.9)]. So $w\in D_{2k}$ iff $S-X\!V-\sum B_{ij}\in\Delta^{2k}$, and the three parts are the three places where a group can lose levels against a ring.

Lemma 8 [on paper] (sufficient conditions). Let $x_i^{E_i}\in\gamma_k$, $E_i=2^{a_i}\ge4$, $k\le4$ (so the pairing lemma of §6.7 holds: $2^sE_iX_i\in\Delta^{2+s}$ for $2+s\le k$). (i) If $g_i=\zeta^{F}s^{F'}$ with $\zeta\in\gamma_k$, $s\in\gamma_{k+1}$, $F=2^{k-2}E_i$, $F'=2^{k-3}E_i$, then $X_i(g_i-1)\in\Delta^{2k}$. (ii) If $v_i\in(\gamma_{k+1})^{2^{k-3}E_i}$, then $X_i(v_i-1)\in\Delta^{2k}$. Proof. $\zeta^F-1\equiv F(\zeta-1)$ modulo $\Delta^{2k}$, and $X_i\cdot F(\zeta-1)=(FX_i)(\zeta-1)\in\Delta^{k+k}$; likewise $X_i(s^{F'}-1)\equiv(F'X_i)(s-1)\in\Delta^{(k-1)+(k+1)}$; the cross term $(\zeta^F-1)(s^{F'}-1)\in\Delta^{2k+1}$. (ii) is the second half of (i). $\square$

This is the "exact pairing": the multiplier is $2^{k-2}$ on the $\gamma_k$-part of $g_i$ (2 for (3.12), 4 at class 5) and $2^{k-3}$ on its $\gamma_{k+1}$-part. Condition (ii) holds as soon as the top layer has elements of large order (in $G$ of (3.12) and of §6.7, $\gamma_c(G)=C_{2^{14}}$).

Lemma 9 [on paper]. Let $G$ be metabelian of class $c$, $k=c-1$, $x_i^{E_i}=P_i\in\gamma_k$, and $E_iE_j\mid A_{ij}$. Then $u_{ij}=1$. Hence $w\ne1$ forces $E_iE_j\nmid A_{ij}$ for some $i<j$ (for powers of two: $A_{ij}<E_iE_j$). Proof. $u_{ij}=[x_i,P_j^{q}]=[x_i,P_j]^q$, $q=A_{ij}/E_j$, since $[x_i,P_j]\in\gamma_c$ is central; and $[x_i,P_j]^{E_i}=[x_i^{E_i},P_j]=[P_i,P_j]=1$ ($\gamma_k\subseteq G'$ is abelian). $\square$

(c) Level count: why a carrier buys one level

Call the certified level of $N\,X_{t_1}\cdots X_{t_m}$ ($N$ a power of two) the largest $\sum_r\ell_r$ over factorisations $N=\prod_rN_r$, where $\ell_r$ is the level that the pairing lemma gives to $N_rX_{t_r}$ (1 if $N_r<E_{t_r}$, else $\min(2+\log_2(N_r/E_{t_r}),k)$). It is a lower bound for the true level; relators can do better.

Lemma 10 [on paper]. Let $E_i\le E_j$, $A=A_{ij}=2^\alpha<E_iE_j$ (the pair of Lemma 9). The certified level of $\binom A2X_j^2X_i$ is at most $k+2=c+1$. Proof. $v_2\binom A2=\alpha-1\le a_i+a_j-2$. Raising $X_j$ to level $\ge2$ costs $\ge a_j$, and then $\le a_i-2<a_i$ is left: the other two factors stay at level 1, total $\le k+2$. Otherwise only $X_i$ is raised, to $\le k$: total $\le k+2$. $\square$

The same count gives $S$ the certified level $k+\min(2+\log_2m,k)$ for multiplier $m$, and $X\!V$ the level $2k$ under Lemma 8(ii). So with the pairing lemma alone the decomposition certifies $w\in D_{c+1}$ at best, and $D_{2c-2}$ needs $c-3$ further levels for the binomial term of the $w$-pair: one at class 4, two at class 5. (d) What a class-5 presentation needs

With $k=c-1=4$, by Lemmas 7–10 [on paper]:

  • (R1) pairing: $g_i\in(\gamma_4)^{4E_i}(\gamma_5)^{2E_i}$ for every $i$, or a cancellation $S\equiv X\!V+\sum B$ modulo $\Delta^8$ not of this kind;

(f) Toward $f_{\rm met}(5)=7$

Lemma 13 [on paper] (quadratic pairing). Let $k\in\{3,4\}$, $G$ of class $\le k+1$, $x^E=P=\prod_ld_l^{e_l}$ with $d_l\in\gamma_k$, $E=2^e\ge4$, $X=x-1$, $s\in\{0,1\}$, $2+s\le k$. Proof. $x^E-1=\sum_{m\ge1}\binom EmX^m$, so $2^{s+3}EX^2=2^{s+3}(P-1)X-\sum_{m\ge2}2^{s+3}\binom EmX^{m+1}$. Modulo $\Delta^{2k}$, $P-1\equiv\sum_le_lD_l$. A term $2^{s+3}e_lD_lX$ is $D_l\cdot\frac{8e_l}E\,(2^sEX)\in\Delta^{k+2+s}$ in the first case (pairing lemma of §6.7: $2^sEX\in\Delta^{2+s}$), and $(d_l^{2^{s+3}e_l}-1)X\in\Delta^{k+2+s}$ up to $\Delta^{2k}$ in the second. (b) The method

Notation: $A_k$ has the degrees $<k$; $J_{k-1}\subseteq A_{k-1}$ is the truncation of $J_k$ (§3.4); $S_k$, $\mathrm{NF}_k$ as above.

Lemma N1 [on paper]. $J_k=\mathrm{span}\{\mu(r)-1:r\in R_0\}+\sum_j\big(J_{k-1}a_j+a_jJ_{k-1}\big)$, the products taken in $A_k$ (also modulo $2^E$). Proof. For $z\in J_{k-1}$ take a lift $\tilde z\in J_k$; $\tilde z a_j=za_j$ in $A_k$ because the degree-$(k-1)$ part of $\tilde z$ dies in the product; so "$\supseteq$". Conversely $J_k$ is spanned by $u(\mu(r)-1)v$ with monomials $u,v$; if $v=v'a_j$, then $u(\mu(r)-1)v=\big(u(\mu(r)-1)v'\big)a_j$ and the bracket is in $J_k$, its truncation in $J_{k-1}$; the same on the left. $\square$

Candidates and the projection. $C_k$: the words of degree 1 and the words $v$ of degree $p\ge2$ with $v[{:}{-1}],v[1{:}]\in S_{k-1}$. Since $S_k\cap(\deg<k-1)=S_{k-1}$ and $S_k$ is closed under prefixes and suffixes, $S_k\subseteq C_k$. For $v\notin C_k$: if $u=v[{:}{-1}]\notin S_{k-1}$, $v\mapsto\mathrm{NF}_{k-1}(u)\,a_l$ (prefix rule); otherwise $v=a_fu'$ with $u'\notin S_{k-1}$, $v\mapsto a_f\,\mathrm{NF}_{k-1}(u')$ (suffix rule). Each step is a congruence modulo $J_k$ ($u-\mathrm{NF}_{k-1}(u)$ is the truncation of an element of $J_k$; the difference is in degree $k-1$ and dies after the shift) and moves to strictly later columns; $\pi_k$ is the result. So $x-\pi_k(x)\in J_k$, $\pi_k$ is the identity on $C_k$, and $$x\in J_k\iff\pi_k(x)\in\pi_k(J_k),\qquad A_k^+/J_k\cong\mathbb Z^{C_k}/\pi_k(J_k)$$ — the index, the free columns and generators are those of the dense form, so the certificate of §6.4 applies unchanged.

Lemma N2 [on paper]. $\pi_k(J_k)$ is generated by $\pi_k$ of: (i) $\mu(r)-1$; (ii) $ta_j$, $a_jt$ for the rows $t$ of the Howell form of $\pi_{k-1}(J_{k-1})$ with a non-unit pivot; (iii) the overlaps $a_j\big(u-\mathrm{NF}_{k-1}(u)\big)$ for $u\notin S_{k-1}$ with $u[{:}{-1}]\in S_{k-1}$ and $a_j\,u[{:}{-1}]\notin S_{k-1}$. Proof. By N1, with the generating set $\{g_u=u-\mathrm{NF}_{k-1}(u):u\notin S_{k-1}\}\cup\{\text{torsion rows}\}$ of $J_{k-1}$ (the reduced Howell form in all columns). $\pi_k(g_ua_j)=0$ by the prefix rule. $\pi_k(a_jg_u)=0$ when $a_j u[{:}{-1}]\in S_{k-1}$ (the suffix rule applies to $a_ju$). If $u=u'a_l$ with $u'\notin S_{k-1}$: $z=g_u-g_{u'}a_l\in J_{k-1}$ lies on columns after $u$, and $a_jg_u=\big(a_jg_{u'}\big)a_l+a_jz$ in $A_k$; the first term is a right shift of an element of $J_{k-1}$ (its $\pi_k$ is in the span of (ii)), and $z$ is a combination of $g_x$, $x>u$, and torsion rows (Howell property); induction downwards on $u$. $\square$

Then $H_k$ is the Howell form of these rows in the columns $C_k$, and $\mathrm{NF}_k(u)$ for $u\notin S_k$ is $\pi_k(u)$ reduced by $H_k$, kept as matrices $T_k[p]$ (rows: non-standard words of degree $p$, columns: candidates of degree $\ge p$) for the prefix and suffix rules of level $k+1$. The truncation property is checked at every level (pivots and valuations below the top degree equal those of $H_{k-1}$).

Implementation [computer]. gpu/stdmono.py run (same output fields as howell_gpu.py batch, plus columns, per-level statistics): relators and tests are built once in $A_n$ (binomial expansion for powers of long words: $n-2$ products instead of binary powering) and truncated per level; $\pi_k$ is two GEMMs per letter with $T_{k-1}$, repeated until no non-candidate column is left; the Howell form and its images modulo $2^{30},2^{20},2$ by howell_gpu.py (numpy or cupy), or with --sparse by gpu/sparse_howell.c: rows inserted one by one, the latest leading column first, each new pivot row reduced by the pivots already there (in generation order and unreduced the fill-in was 12 times larger: c4a $n=6$, $210\,497$ nonzeros against $17\,162$; $n=7$, $16.9$ M and 23 min in the C insertion, stopped). --digest rebuilds the reduced form in all $M$ columns (rows of $H_n$, and $u+\mathrm{reduce}(-\pi_n(u))$ for the other words) and prints howell_gpu.digest. 400 random matrices: the C form and howell_gpu.howell agree in pivots, index, images and membership.

7. Literature, and whether the method is known

A dagger † marks a reference that was not checked against the source.

  • The formula $D_n(F/R)=F\cap(1+\mathfrak r+\mathfrak f^n)$ and the Magnus embedding are classics of the theory of dimension subgroups: R. Mikhailov, I. B. S. Passi, Lower Central and Dimension Series of Groups, Lecture Notes in Math. 1952 (2009), ch. 1 (the exact section not checked) †; N. Gupta, Free group rings, Contemp. Math. 66 (1987) †. The truncated isomorphism (a) is a special case of Magnus's theorem (W. Magnus, 1937 †).
  • [BM23] L. Bartholdi, R. Mikhailov, Group and Lie algebra filtrations and homotopy groups of spheres, arXiv:1805.10894 (checked through the arXiv API); journal version J. Topology 16 (2023) 822–853 †. Its ancillary file 3group.gap gives a pc-presentation of a 3-group of order $3^{494}$ on 7 generators with a check of the relations and the class in GAP. There are no computations in the group ring: the proof is theoretical.
  • [IMP26] V. Ionin, R. Mikhailov, T. Petrov, Dimension subrings of Lie rings, arXiv:2610.02042 (checked through the arXiv API).
  • [HMP07] M. Hartl, R. Mikhailov, I. B. S. Passi, Dimension quotients, arXiv:0803.3290 (checked through the arXiv API, which gives J. Indian Math. Soc. (N.S.), special volume for the centenary of the IMS (1907–2007), 63–107). It contains no explicit presentation of the Rips group.
  • ModIsom (B. Eick, a GAP package): NilpotentQuotientOfFpAlgebra, the nilpotent quotient of a finitely presented associative algebra. According to its documentation it works over a finite field (the example and the field fld of the result), that is, it gives $\mathbb F_p[G]/\Delta^n_{\mathbb F_p}$, the Jennings series $M_n$, not the integral $D_n$. Support for $\mathbb Z$ was not checked in the sources †. The algorithm: B. Eick, Computing nilpotent quotients of associative algebras and algebras satisfying a polynomial identity, IJAC 21 (2011) †.
  • LAGUNA: modular group algebras $\mathbb F_p[G]$, again Jennings, not $\mathbb Z$.
  • The same method was implemented independently (unpublished); the comparison is in §5.

Conclusion. The identity $\mathbb Z[G]/\Delta^n\cong A_n/J_n$ is classical. Its computational use for the integral $D_n$, that is, linear algebra over $\mathbb Z/p^E$ or $\mathbb Z$ in $A_n$ of a size that does not depend on $|G|$, we have not found in the literature we searched. The search was not exhaustive.
