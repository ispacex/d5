Remark 6.7
#

The exponent hypothesis is used in three places: in (C) (for \(d(j)\ge 3d(i)\) Tahara’s condition is empty and terms \(u_{ij}[x_i^{d(i)},x_j^3]\) with \(x_j^3\notin \gamma _2\) appear), in the choice of basis (Tahara’s (D), (E) hold only modulo \((d(i),e(k))\), \((d(i),f(l))\)), and in (iii). Theorems 6.8 and 6.9 below remove the first two: what they need is that \(\gamma _2/\gamma _3\) has exponent \(3\). At exponent \(9\) the method breaks in several places: identity (iii) may fail, and \(\gamma _2/\gamma _3\) is no longer elementary, so the \(Y_i\) of these proofs cannot always be taken as basis elements, and (D)–(F) are taken modulo numbers that may equal \(9\). Theorem 6.10 below shows that exponent \(9\) by itself is not an obstruction: the method still works if \(\gamma _4^3=1\) and \(\gamma _2^9\subseteq \gamma _4\). As for (iii): in a group of class \(4\), for \(x\in Q\), \(a\in \gamma _2\) and \(c=[x,a]\), the exact formula is \([x^3,a]=c^{x^2}c^xc=[x,a]^3[x,a,x]^3\), as \([x,a,x,x]\in \gamma _5\), while \([x,a^3]=[x,a]^3\) always holds, as \([\gamma _3,\gamma _2]=1\); so (iii) holds if and only if \([x,a,x]^3=1\), which is automatic when \(\gamma _2/\gamma _3\) has exponent \(3\). For \(Q=\langle x,y\mid x^9,y^9\rangle /\gamma _5\), of order \(3^{15}\) and class \(4\), with \(\gamma _2/\gamma _3\cong C_9\), a computation gives, for \(a=[x,y]\), \([x,a,x]^3\ne 1\) and so \([x^3,a]\ne [x,a]^3\), and also \([x^9,y]=1\ne [x,y]^9\). (This \(Q\) has two generators, so it is metabelian, see the proof of Lemma 6.1, and is not a counterexample; it only shows that these identities fail.) A computational check of the theorem: \(169\) random non-metabelian groups of class \(4\) with \(Q/\gamma _2\cong C_3^3\) or \(C_3^4\), of orders \(3^{10}\)–\(3^{79}\), \(29\) of them with \(\Omega _1(Z)\cap Q''\cap M_5\ne 1\): identity (iii) holds and \(D_5=1\) in all of them (truncated Magnus algebra modulo \(3^{19}\) with the exactness certificate).

Proof ▶

See the paper. Computer-assisted; not formalized.

Remark 6.13
#

  1. For \(p=2\) the theorem fails, as a computation shows: SmallGroup\((64,138)\) has type \((3,2,1)\), while its \(2\)-covering group has order \(2^{13}\), class \(4\) and \(|P^{*\prime \prime }|=2\); likewise SmallGroup\((128,i)\), \(i=522,599,621,1755\), and \(12\) groups of order \(2^8\).

  2. A computational check: the hypothesis holds for \(13\, 315\) groups of order \(3^8\) and \(2\, 754\) of order \(7^7\), and \(P^{*\prime \prime }=1\) in all of them.

  3. The theorem does not say that such \(P\) are terminal. Of the \(7\, 824\) groups of order \(3^8\), type \((5,2,1)\) and rank \(5\) (SmallGroup\((3^8,i)\), \(1\, 387\, 558\le i\le 1\, 395\, 381\)), a computation finds that \(190\) have nucleus of order \(3\), e.g. SmallGroup\((81,9)\times 3^{1+2}_+\times C_3\), and that all of their descendants are metabelian of type \((5,2,1,1)\). Theorem 6.14 below says exactly which groups of type \((d,2,1)\) with \(P/\gamma _2\) elementary abelian are nonterminal, and on this block it selects exactly the \(190\) (by computation). Its conditions (N1)–(N3) and (E) alone hold for the \(190\) and also for \(573\) terminal groups; the Jacobi condition (J) separates the two.

Theorem 6.14
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • GroupDimension.nonterminal_necessary
  • GroupDimension.nonterminal_sufficient
  • GroupDimension.nonterminal_iff

Let \(p\) be odd and \(P\) a finite \(p\)-group of type \((d,2,1)\) with \(P/\gamma _2(P)\) elementary abelian, and write \([a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab\). Then \(P\) is nonterminal (it has an immediate descendant, equivalently its nucleus is nontrivial) if and only if (N1) \(|Z(P)\cap \gamma _2(P)|=p^2\), (N2) \(\gamma _2(P)\) is elementary abelian, (N3) \(P^p\le Z(P)\), and (N4) the linear system (E)+(J) below over \(\mathbb F_p\) has a solution with \(c=1\).

To state (N4), assume (N1)–(N3) and choose a generator \(w\) of \(\gamma _3(P)\), elements \(v\in Z(P)\cap \gamma _2(P)\setminus \gamma _3(P)\) and \(u\in \gamma _2(P)\setminus Z(P)\), and generators \(x_1,\dots ,x_d\) of \(P\). Then \(\gamma _2(P)=\langle u\rangle \times \langle v\rangle \times \langle w\rangle \), \(x_i^p\in \langle v,w\rangle \), and

\[ [x_i,x_j]=u^{\omega _1(i,j)}v^{\omega _2(i,j)}w^{\omega _3(i,j)},\qquad x_i^p=v^{\alpha _i}w^{\beta _i},\qquad [u,x_i]=w^{f_i}, \]

with alternating \(\omega _r\) and \(f\ne 0\). The unknowns are \(c,\mu _u,\mu _v\in \mathbb F_p\) and \(\eta ,\sigma \in \mathbb F_p^d\); let \(\varepsilon _3=1\) and \(\varepsilon _p=0\) for \(p\ge 5\): for all \(1\le i,j\le d\), including \(i=j\),

\[ \text{(E)}\qquad \alpha _j\eta _i+c\, \beta _jf_i+\mu _u\, \omega _1(i,j)+\mu _v\, \omega _2(i,j)+\varepsilon _p\, c\, f_j^2\, \omega _1(i,j)=0, \]

and for all \(1\le i{\lt}j{\lt}k\le d\), with \((a,b,e)\) running over the cyclic shifts of \((i,j,k)\),

\[ \text{(J)}\qquad \textstyle \sum _{(a,b,e)}\bigl(\omega _1(a,b)\, \sigma _e+\omega _2(a,b)\, \eta _e+c\, \omega _3(a,b)\, f_e\bigr)=0. \]

Lean: GroupDimension.nonterminal_necessary, GroupDimension.nonterminal_sufficient, GroupDimension.nonterminal_iff.

“Nonterminal” is stated in the form of Lemma C.1 (a central extension by a subgroup of order p with γ_4 ≠ 1); Lemma C.1, that this means having immediate descendants, is not formalized; the normal form of Step 3 is given, not constructed.

Remark 6.15
#

  1. A computational check. For the \(7\, 824\) groups of Remark 6.13(3) and for all groups of order \(p^7\), type \((4,2,1)\) and rank \(4\) (i.e. with \(P/\gamma _2\) elementary) with \(p=3,5,7\) (\(932\), \(1\, 460\), \(2\, 232\) groups), the nucleus was computed in two ways: with ANUPQ, and with GAP’s own \(p\)-quotient of \(F/[R,F]R^p\). The two agree, and \(N\ne 1\) exactly for the groups satisfying (N1)–(N4): \(190\), \(102\), \(110\), \(118\) groups. For each of these, the pc-presentation of the proof built from the solution was checked to be consistent, with \(\gamma _4\ne 1\), and to map onto \(P\); so for these groups sufficiency does not rest on the consistency test. Without the term \(c\, \omega _3f\) in (J) the criterion gives wrong answers both ways: on the block it accepts \(295\) terminal groups and rejects \(13\) nonterminal ones. Code: compute/magnus.

  2. The system is linear and homogeneous in \((c,\eta ,\sigma ,\mu _u,\mu _v)\), so (N4) is the solvability of an inhomogeneous linear system. The term with \(\varepsilon _p\) is \(\binom p3[x_j,x_i,x_j,x_j]\), the only place where \(p=3\) differs from \(p\ge 5\). Whether (N4) holds does not depend on the choices made, since it is equivalent to nonterminality.

  3. The criterion assumes \(P/\gamma _2\) elementary abelian, i.e. \(d(P)=d\); this implies that \(P\) has \(p\)-class \(3\), but not conversely. It covers all groups of order \(3^8\), type \((5,2,1)\) and rank \(5\), i.e. all possible parents in Theorem 6.16, but not all groups of type \((5,2,1)\): for instance \(M\times (C_9\rtimes C_9)\), with \(M\) of maximal class and order \(81\) and \(C_9\rtimes C_9\) non-abelian, has order \(3^8\), type \((5,2,1)\) and rank \(4\). Groups of type \((d,2,1)\) with \(P/\gamma _2\) not elementary are not treated here.
