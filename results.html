LeanReduction: $D_5(G)\subseteq\gamma_4(G)$ for all groups $\iff$ $D_5(Q)=1$ for all finite $2$-groups of class $3$. For finite $G$ the order does not grow: $2$-groups up to the $2$-part of $|G|$ suffice. Proved in Lean without hypotheses: Passi's theorem ($D_4^2\subseteq\gamma_4$), Gruenberg's theorem and $D_3=\gamma_3$ are proved there too.Prop. 2.1, Rem. 2.3; Lean

A counterexample, if any, has order $\ge2^{12}$. LeanCentral products: $D_n(H\circ E)=D_n(H)$ for $E\in\{D_8,Q_8\}$ and $t\in\gamma_2(H)$, and for $E=M_{2^{k+2}}$ under a condition on $g^{-2}s$ (e.g. $s=g^2$). computerRips's group (order $2^{38}$, from Tahara's 1978 presentation): $D_4=\{1,y_1^{128}\}$, and every proper subgroup has $D_4=1$; $26\,147$ random quotients (with repetitions) also have $D_4=1$, and a heuristic hill climb over the exponents of Tahara's presentation found nothing below $2^{38}$. The sections checked give no calibration below $2^{38}$ (proper sections $H/N$ with $H\lt G$, $N\ne1$ not checked; code).§5
computer$3$-torsion: the Bartholdi–Mikhailov $3$-group with $D_7\ne\gamma_7$ has order $3^{494}$, but its three quotients by central subgroups of order $3$ not containing the witness $\omega$ have order $3^{493}$ and $D_7\ne\gamma_7$, so $3^{494}$ is not the least order of a $3$-group with $D_7\ne\gamma_7$; every proper quotient of these three groups kills $\omega$. That $\omega\in D_7$ follows from Bartholdi–Mikhailov's Prop. 9.2 via the map (not onto) from their group (9.2) to $G$. Subgroups of either group, and new witnesses in quotients, not checked (code).§5
computer$3$-torsion: a $3$-group with $D_n\ne\gamma_n$ (for some $n$) has order $\ge3^{10}$.
