Definitions

dimension subgroups

$D_n(G)=G\cap(1+\Delta^n)=\{g\in G: g-1\in\Delta^n\}$, where $\Delta$ is the augmentation ideal of $\mathbb Z[G]$. Always $\gamma_n(G)\subseteq D_n(G)$; for $n\le3$ this is an equality, for $n=4$ in the general case it is not (Rips). The quotient $D_n(G)/\gamma_n(G)$ is abelian (Gupta–Kuzmin), and its exponent is bounded by a function of $n$ alone (Sjögren).

augmentation ideal

The ideal $\Delta=\Delta(G)$ of the ring $\mathbb Z[G]$, the kernel of the augmentation $\sum a_g g\mapsto\sum a_g$; as an abelian group it is generated by the elements $g-1$, $g\in G$. Its powers define the dimension subgroups: $D_n(G)=\{g\in G: g-1\in\Delta^n\}$.

lower central series

$\gamma_1(G)=G$, $\gamma_{n+1}(G)=[\gamma_n(G),G]$ — the subgroup generated by the commutators $[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$ with $a\in\gamma_n(G)$, $b\in G$. The nilpotency class (degree) of $G$ is the least $c$ with $\gamma_{c+1}(G)=1$.

Machine-translated from Russian

commutator

$[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab$, so $ab=ba[a,b]$. This is the convention of the paper on $D_5$ (explicitly in Theorem 6.17 and Appendix C).

Jennings subgroups

For a finite $p$-group $Q$: $M_n(Q)=Q\cap(1+\Delta_{\mathbb F_p}(Q)^n)$, dimension subgroups modulo $p$; by Jennings (and Lazard) $M_n=\prod_{ip^j\ge n}\gamma_i^{p^j}$. Since $\mathbb Z[Q]\to\mathbb F_p[Q]$ maps $\Delta^n$ to $\Delta_{\mathbb F_p}^n$, always $D_n(Q)\subseteq M_n(Q)$.

lower exponent-p central series

For the $p$-group $Q$: $P_1(Q)=Q$, $P_{i+1}(Q)=[P_i(Q),Q]\,P_i(Q)^p$. The $p$-class of $Q$ is the smallest $c$ with $P_{c+1}(Q)=1$. Always $\gamma_i\subseteq P_i$, and $P_2=\Phi(Q)$.

p-covering group

For a finite $p$-group $P=F/R$, where $F$ is a free group of rank $d(P)$: $P^*=F/[R,F]R^p$ (O'Brien). Its subgroup $M=R/[R,F]R^p\le Z(P^*)$ is the multiplier (elementary abelian, $P^*/M\cong P$), and $N=P_{c+1}(P^*)\le M$ is the kernel ($c$ is the $p$-class of $P$). The immediate descendants of step 1 of the group $P$ are $P^*/U$, where $U<M$ has index $p$ and $N\not\le U$.

multiplier

The subgroup $M=R/[R,F]R^p$ of the $p$-covering group $P^*=F/[R,F]R^p$ of the group $P=F/R$: central, elementary abelian, $P^*/M\cong P$.

Machine-translated from Russian

nucleus

$N=P_{c+1}(P^*)\le M$, where $P^*$ is the $p$-covering group of the $p$-group $P$, $M$ is its multiplier, $c$ is the $p$-class of $P$. $P$ has immediate descendants (it is nonterminal) if and only if $N\ne1$.

immediate descendant

If the $p$-class of the $p$-group $Q$ is equal to $c\ge2$, then $Q$ is an immediate descendant of the group $Q/P_c(Q)$ of step $\log_p|P_c(Q)|$. The descendants of step 1 of the group $P$ are the $P^*/U$, where $P^*$ is a $p$-covering group and $U$ is a subgroup of index $p$ of the multiplier that does not contain the nucleus. The algorithm for generating $p$-groups (ANUPQ) enumerates them up to isomorphism.

candidate

$2$-group $Q$ of nilpotency class $3$ with cyclic center, whose involution $n$ lies in $\gamma_3(Q)\cap M_5(Q)$, and with $2$-class $c\ge4$ with $P_c(Q)=\langle n\rangle$. By Lemma 5.2, a finite $2$-group of nilpotency class $3$ with $D_5(Q)\ne1$, for which $D_5(Q/N)=1$ for all central $N$ of order 2, is a candidate. In the proof of Theorem 6.19, the candidates are descendants of step 1 of order $3^9$ of nilpotency class 4 with $Q''\ne1$, cyclic center, and $z\in Q''\cap M_5$.

type (of the lower central series)

For a group $Q$ of nilpotency class $c$ — a set $(a_1,\dots,a_c)$ with $p^{a_i}=|\gamma_i(Q)/\gamma_{i+1}(Q)|$; for example, $(3,2,2,1)$ or $(3,3,1,1)$ for $p=3$.

D5 question

Kourovka Notebook, Problem 20.84 (question 1.1 of the article): is it true $D_5(G)\subseteq\gamma_4(G)$ for every group $G$? Equivalently: $D_5(Q)=1$ for all finite $2$-groups of nilpotency class $3$ (Proposition 2.1). A related open question is whether $3$-torsion can occur in $D_5/\gamma_5$ (§7, item 2).

Question 1.1 Kourovka Notebook, Problem 20.84 open

Is it true that $D_5(G)\subseteq\gamma_4(G)$ for every group $G$?

Proposition 2.1 Reduction to 2-groups of class 3 paper

The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. $D_5(G)\subseteq\gamma_4(G)$ for every group $G$;
  2. $D_5(M)=1$ for every metabelian group $M$ of nilpotency class at most $3$;
  3. $D_5(Q)=1$ for every finite metabelian $2$-group $Q$ of nilpotency class exactly $3$.

(Every group of class $\le3$ is metabelian, so the word "metabelian" can be omitted — Remark 2.2.)

Machine-translated from Russian

Lemma 5.2 Minimal counterexample paper

Let $Q$ be a finite $2$-group of nilpotency class $3$ and $D_5(Q/N)=1$ for every central subgroup $N$ of order $2$. If $D_5(Q)\ne1$, then

  • (a) the center $Z(Q)$ is cyclic and $D_5(Q)=\Omega_1(Z(Q))=\langle n\rangle$ has order $2$;
  • (b) $n\in\gamma_3(Q)\cap M_5(Q)$;
  • (c) the $2$-class $c$ of the group $Q$ is at least $4$ and $P_c(Q)=\langle n\rangle$;
  • (d) $Q$ is an immediate descendant of step $1$ of the group $R=Q/\langle n\rangle$ whose nilpotency class is $2$ or $3$, $2$-class $c-1\ge3$ and $\gamma_3(R)$ is cyclic.

Machine-translated from Russian

Proposition 5.3 Order 2¹⁰ computer

$D_5(Q)=1$ for every $2$-group $Q$ of order $2^{10}$ and nilpotency class $3$ (all of them are metabelian). By computer.

Theorem 5.4 Groups of small order computer

  1. $D_5(G)\subseteq\gamma_4(G)$ for every finite group $G$ whose Sylow $2$-subgroup has order at most $2^{10}$; in particular, for every group of order less than $2048$.
  2. $D_n(G)=\gamma_n(G)$ for all $n$ for every finite group $G$ of order less than $512$.
  3. $D_5(G)/\gamma_5(G)$ has no $3$-torsion for every finite group $G$ whose Sylow $3$-subgroup has order at most $3^7$; moreover, $D_5(Q)=\gamma_5(Q)$ for every $3$-group $Q$ of order at most $3^7$ (up to $3^9$ — Theorem 6.19).

By computer.

Machine-translated from Russian

Proposition 5.7 Candidate rank computer

$D_5(Q)=1$ for every $2$-group $Q$ of order $2^{11}$, nilpotency class $3$ and rank $d(Q)\ge8$. More generally, every candidate $Q$ satisfies $|\Phi(Q)|\ge16$, that is, $d(Q)\le\log_2|Q|-4$.

The second statement was proved on paper (and in Lean); the first is derived from it and Proposition 5.3, so it was obtained by computer.

Proposition 5.10 Order 2¹¹ computer

$D_5(Q)=1$ for every $2$-group $Q$ of order $2^{11}$ of nilpotency class $3$. Consequently, a counterexample to Question 1.1 among finite $2$-groups of nilpotency class $3$ has order at least $2^{12}$. By computer.

Theorem 6.9 γ₂/γ₃ of exponent 3 paper

Let $Q$ be a finite $3$-group in which $\gamma_2(Q)/\gamma_3(Q)$ has exponent at most $3$, i.e. $[x^3,y]\in\gamma_3(Q)$ for all $x,y\in Q$. Then $D_5(Q)=\gamma_5(Q)$, whatever the invariants of $Q/\gamma_2(Q)$. More generally, $D_5(G)=\gamma_5(G)$ for any group $G$ in which $G/\gamma_2(G)$ is a finite $3$-group and $\gamma_2(G)/\gamma_3(G)$ has exponent at most $3$.

Theorem 6.10 γ₄³ = 1 and γ₂⁹ ⊆ γ₄ paper

Let $Q$ be a finite $3$-group of nilpotency class $\le4$ satisfying (H1) the exponent of $\gamma_4(Q)$ divides $3$ and (H2) $a^9\in\gamma_4(Q)$ for every $a\in\gamma_2(Q)$. Then $D_5(Q)=1$. More generally, $D_5(G)=\gamma_5(G)$ for every group $G$ such that $G/\gamma_2(G)$ is a finite $3$-group, $\gamma_4(G)^3\subseteq\gamma_5(G)$ and $\gamma_2(G)^9\subseteq\gamma_4(G)$.

Machine-translated from Russian

Theorem 6.11 γ₄³ ⊆ γ₅ paper

Let $Q$ be a finite $3$-group of nilpotency class $\le4$ and $\gamma_4(Q)^3=1$. Then $D_5(Q)=1$. More generally, $D_5(G)=\gamma_5(G)$ for any group $G$ for which $G/\gamma_2(G)$ is a finite $3$-group and $\gamma_4(G)^3\subseteq\gamma_5(G)$.

Machine-translated from Russian

Corollary 6.12 D₅ ⊆ γ₄³ paper

Let $Q$ be a finite $3$-group of nilpotency class $\le4$. Then $D_5(Q)\subseteq\gamma_4(Q)^3$. More generally, $D_5(G)\subseteq\gamma_4(G)^3\gamma_5(G)$ for any group $G$ whose $G/\gamma_2(G)$ is a finite $3$-group.

Machine-translated from Russian

Theorem 6.19 3-groups of order up to 3⁹ computer

$D_5(Q)=\gamma_5(Q)$ for every $3$-group $Q$ of order at most $3^9$. Consequently, $D_5(G)/\gamma_5(G)$ has no $3$-torsion for every finite group $G$ whose Sylow $3$-subgroup has order at most $3^9$. By computer.

Open question §7, item 2 3-torsion in D₅/γ₅ open

Does $3$-torsion exist in $D_5(G)/\gamma_5(G)$ for some group $G$, i.e., is the Tahara bound $6$ attained for the exponent of $D_5/\gamma_5$?

Known: there is no $3$-torsion if the Sylow $3$-subgroup has order $\le3^9$ (Theorem 6.19), and $D_5=\gamma_5$ if $G/\gamma_2$ is a finite $3$-group and $\gamma_4^3\subseteq\gamma_5$ (Theorem 6.11). The minimal $3$-group with $D_5\ne\gamma_5$ has order $\ge3^{10}$, class $4$, cyclic center, $\gamma_2/\gamma_3$ of exponent $\ge9$, and cyclic $\gamma_4\le Z(Q)$ of order $\ge9$.

Open question §7, item 3 Group analogue of Theorem C open

Group analogue of the Ionin–Mikhailov–Petrov theorem C: is it true that $D_{2c-1}(G)=1$ for every metabelian group $G$ of nilpotency class $\le c$? For $c=3$ this is Question 1.1. The question reduces to finite metabelian $p$-groups; for odd $p$ the answer is positive for all $c$ (Gupta, 1991), so only the case $p=2$, $c\ge3$ remains open.
